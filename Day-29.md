53/100. Grade D. 

That's what I scored when I tried to save a $1.9B company from collapse.

Operation Lifeline is a Supply Chain Crisis Lab that puts you in the COO seat. No tutorials. No safety net. Just a live crisis and a ticking clock.

Here's what happened to me:

Day 0 — My single-source supplier filed Chapter 11 overnight.
Day 1 — 60% of raw materials: gone. No backup. No plan.
Day 12 — Stockout. Production slowing. Customers at risk.

I had budget for exactly 3 crisis actions. I had to choose between:
→ Emergency air freight ($4.1M/week)
→ Activating backup suppliers ($2.8M setup)
→ Rationing to top 20% of customers
→ Rerouting production across 4 countries
→ Buying spot-market inventory at premium prices

Every choice had a tradeoff. Every delay had a cost.

After it all? My cost control cratered to 42%. Profit margin: 47%. Customer satisfaction: 55%.

But the real lesson wasn't in the score.

It was this: The decisions that save you in a crisis are made months before the crisis arrives.

Dual sourcing. Buffer stock. Supplier trust. Real-time visibility.
Not nice-to-haves. Survival infrastructure.

Try it. It'll change how you think about operations.
